## Governor and resource bounds

46. **[high · U] LIFE.md §10: fleet load is observed but not reserved against long actions.** A fleet near its 3000-second work threshold can admit a long action on a node with little prior LIFE work. Do not admit an action whose declared occupancy cannot fit the remaining fleet allowance, unless a separately defined maintenance exception applies. Refresh the governor during long-running activity, and specify which already-running actions are cancelled or allowed to finish when the fleet threshold is reached.”
47. **[high · U] LIFE.md §10: incomplete observations can be mistaken for observed quiet.** A readable status list does not prove valid CPU data for every relevant node or continuous quiet throughout a long interval. The current quiet-time rule does not fully define missing nodes, missing CPU readings, or gaps between samples. **Replace:** “Quiet credit requires two valid observations separated by no more than TELEMETRY_REFRESH, complete required telemetry for the relevant nodes, and no conflicting execution reservation. Missing or stale observations provide no quiet credit. Distinguish explicitly offline, missing, and malformed node records; only explicit offline observations contribute to offline-move eligibility.”
48. **[high · U] db/life.sql:node_holds; stop_everything: independent holds overwrite one another.** There is one row per node, and STOP uses INSERT OR REPLACE. The representation cannot preserve an unresolved-job hold alongside an orphan or infrastructure hold; replacement can shorten an existing restriction. **Replace:** “Store holds by (node, cause, subject) and evaluate their union. Creating or renewing one hold cannot shorten or delete another. Release only the specifically resolved cause. A node becomes eligible only when all applicable holds and execution reservations are cleared.”
49. **[medium · D] JOB_UNKNOWN_HOLD: an unresolved job becomes eligible merely through age.** A one-year timeout contradicts “until the listing shows it ended or the operator releases it.” **Replace:** “Unknown-job holds have no automatic expiry. Store an explicit indefinite hold and resolve it only through verified termination or an audited operator orphan-resolution command. Do not approximate an indefinite condition with a large finite timestamp.”
50. **[high · U] Procs.run; LIFE.md §§10, 13: local disk consumption is not bounded during execution.** Memory limits and output-file size polling do not limit arbitrary writes to the checkout or temporary HOME. A candidate can exhaust disk before the next free-space check. Candidate output goes through non-seekable channels with cumulative byte accounting and bounded capture. Limit breach terminates the whole execution cgroup and invalidates its result; post-run cleanup is not the enforcement mechanism.”
51. **[high · D] LIFE.md §18 GAP 7: benchmark resource limits remain per process, not per job.** A trusted template can accidentally spawn many children, each receiving its own address-space and file-size allowance. The acknowledged gap still permits aggregate memory, task, and disk exhaustion. **Replace:** “A benchmark job is admitted only when its trusted remote supervisor establishes job-wide memory, task, CPU, storage, and lifetime limits before executing the template. All descendants remain contained and are removed before the node reservation is released. Until that platform capability exists, benchmark execution is disabled.”
52. **[medium · U] LIFE.md §§3, 18 GAP 14: remote artifact lifetime and quota are undefined.** Mirrors and per-run worktrees accumulate outside LIFE’s accounting. “Operator cleanup” supplies no trigger, ownership proof, or required capacity reserve. **Replace:** “Record every remote artifact with workspace ID, run ID, node, size, and lifecycle state. Before provisioning, reserve remote disk headroom.
